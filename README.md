# HR-Analytics
HR Analytics: Employee Attrition &amp; Satisfaction and Strategic Workforce Insights for Retention Management
Project Overview
This project analyzes employee data for an organization of 1,470 employees to identify the root causes of attrition. By examining demographics, job satisfaction, and education backgrounds, this dashboard provides HR leaders with data-driven insights to reduce turnover (currently at 16.12%) and improve workplace culture.

Key Questions Addressed:
Which departments and age groups are most prone to leaving?

Is there a correlation between job role and low satisfaction ratings?

How does education background impact attrition rates?

Are there gender-based disparities in retention?

Executive Summary of Findings
1. Headcount & Retention KPIs
Total Workforce: 1,470 Employees.

Attrition Rate: 16.12% (237 total departures).

Average Age: 37 years.

2. Attrition Hotspots
Departmental Impact: The R&D Department sees the highest attrition by volume (56.12% of all departures), while Sales follows at 38.82%.

Age Demographics: Employees aged 25–34 represent the largest portion of the workforce and show significant attrition activity.

Education Background: Employees with a background in Life Sciences and Medical fields are leaving at higher rates compared to other disciplines.

3. Satisfaction Matrix
The Job Satisfaction Ratings table reveals that Sales Executives and Research Scientists have the highest frequency of "Level 1" (Low) satisfaction ratings, signaling a need for role-specific interventions.

Recommendations:
1. R&D Engagement: Conduct stay-interviews within the R&D department to understand why it accounts for over 56% of attrition.

2. Sales Role Re-design: Address the low job satisfaction in Sales Executive roles through clearer commission structures or better work-life balance.

3. Early Career Support: Focus retention programs on the 25–34 age group, as they represent the highest "flight risk" in the organization.
